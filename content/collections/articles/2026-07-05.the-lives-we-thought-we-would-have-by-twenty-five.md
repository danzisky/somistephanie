At seventeen, I could describe my twenty-five-year-old self in precise detail: the career, the certainty, the apartment with the right kind of light. She was fully formed, fully arrived, entirely unbothered by doubt.

She does not exist, and I have had to grieve her a little before I could properly meet the woman who did arrive instead—messier, slower, more real.

> The imagined timeline was never a promise. It was only ever a guess, made by someone who hadn't lived the years yet.

Making peace with this distance has not felt like giving up on my younger ambitions. It has felt like finally negotiating with them, honestly, on the terms of an actual life rather than an imagined one.

## An unfolding life, not a missed one

The life I have does not match the one I sketched at seventeen, and I no longer think that means I have fallen behind. It means the sketch was only ever a starting point, not a blueprint.

I am learning to measure this life by its own unfolding, not by how faithfully it resembles a guess I made before I knew any better.
