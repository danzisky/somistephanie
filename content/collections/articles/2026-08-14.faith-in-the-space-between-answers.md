For a long time, I believed faith meant having an answer ready for every question. I have since learned that some of the most faithful seasons of my life have been the ones without answers at all.

Waiting, it turns out, is not the absence of belief. It is often where belief is quietly built—in the space between the question asked and the answer that has not yet come.

> Faith has stopped being about certainty for me. It has become about staying, even when I cannot see clearly.

I no longer read my doubts as evidence against belief. They feel, instead, like the honest texture of a faith that is allowed to be lived rather than performed.

## Making room for the difficult questions

The questions I once tried to silence have become companions instead of threats. I ask them slowly, without rushing towards resolution, trusting that the asking itself is part of a life of faith.

What I am left with is not a tidier belief system, but a more spacious one—one that can hold uncertainty without collapsing, and can wait for answers without demanding them on a schedule of my choosing.
