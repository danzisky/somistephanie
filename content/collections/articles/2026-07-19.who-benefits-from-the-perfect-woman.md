## Refusing the performance

The perfect woman is a familiar character. She is endlessly patient, effortlessly composed, generous without needing to be asked twice. She is also, on close inspection, exhausting to sustain and strangely convenient for everyone but herself.

I have started asking a simpler question than "how do I become her?" I am asking instead: who actually benefits when I try?

> Perfection rarely serves the woman performing it. It tends to serve whoever finds her most convenient that way.

The expectation of perfection asks women to be endlessly accommodating, rarely inconvenient and never quite finished improving. It is a standard that, conveniently, keeps us too busy correcting ourselves to question the standard itself.

## Refusing the performance

Refusing perfection has not made me careless. It has made me honest—about my limits, my needs and the parts of myself I no longer intend to sand down for anyone else's comfort.

There is a freedom in being unfinished on purpose. It returns the question of who I am for to the only person who should have ever been asking it: me.
