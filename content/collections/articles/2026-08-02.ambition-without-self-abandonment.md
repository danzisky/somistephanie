Somewhere along the way, I absorbed the idea that being taken seriously meant becoming smaller—quieter opinions, softer softness, an ambition worn like an apology.

I have spent the last few years unlearning that. Ambition, I now believe, does not require the abandonment of the person doing the striving. Discipline and identity are not opposing forces.

> Success has never once asked me to disappear. That instruction came from somewhere else entirely.

The rooms I have wanted to be in were never actually asking me to erase myself to enter them. That expectation was mine to release, not theirs to withdraw.

## Holding ambition and softness at once

I am learning to bring my full self into rooms built for ambition: the tenderness, the humour, the parts of me that do not fit neatly into "professional." Discipline can still be rigorous without requiring self-abandonment as its price.

What I am building now is a version of ambition that does not cost me myself—one where the goals grow, but so, stubbornly, does the person chasing them.
