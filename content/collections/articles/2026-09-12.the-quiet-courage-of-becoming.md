There is something about being young that creates an almost invulnerability to ageing. It allows us to exist in the indolent bliss of youth, wrapped in the quiet delusion that time will always wait for us.

As I journey through my twenties, the insistence of time has become an inescapable reality. Cracks have begun to form in that naïve, unchallenged way of thinking, allowing the insecurities of growing older to seep in. Through this realisation, however, I have begun to unearth a new kind of authenticity towards ageing.

> Confidence must become more intentional. I have to consciously choose boldness.

My self-belief must now flow from within. I am who I believe myself to be—not what a societal age chart dictates my timeline should look like. The person I am becoming cannot be measured only by deadlines reached, titles collected or milestones made visible to other people.

## A timeline is not a life

I embrace ageing, but specifically my inimitable path towards it: accepting where I am and deeming it enough. I am denouncing the belief that my age is tethered to my achievements, and I refuse to downplay my successes simply because an expected deadline has passed.

There is no single age by which a woman must become accomplished, healed, certain or complete. A life does not become late simply because it unfolds differently from the version we once imagined.

Perhaps growing older is not the closing of possibility, but the steady opening of perspective. The gift is not endless time. It is learning to inhabit the time that is ours with honesty.
